    The highlighted four are the words this workbench accepts (yes / no / left / right). The model also hears the others, including stop and go, but they are reported as rejected here. Wiring more words into Wall Tones is app work and needs no model retraining.

    Scores are model confidence outputs, not measured accuracy. Actual recognition quality on a Quest headset still needs on-device testing, which is what this page is for.

    WHAT TO CHECK ON QUEST
    1. Open this page in Quest Browser and allow the microphone when asked.
    2. Prepare the local receiver. It should report the WebGL backend and a loaded model.
    3. Listen, then say yes, no, left, right. Each word should be accepted with a high score.
    4. Try stop or a digit: the model should show it as the top class, rejected by the workbench.
    5. Optionally compare the other recognition methods from the selector if this browser offers them. On-device speech is feature-detected per browser; whether Quest Browser exposes it is unknown until tested. The browser speech service may send audio to the browser vendor.

    Standalone lab page. In local mode, TensorFlow.js 4.22.0 and speech-commands 0.5.4 are vendored under vendor/ and the 18-word BROWSER_FFT model is served from this same origin under model/, so local recognition makes no third-party requests at test time. The browser speech-service method is different: it may send audio to the browser vendor, and the on-device method is offered only when feature detection says the browser supports it.
